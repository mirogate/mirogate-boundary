AI privacy needs two tests:

What can your detector miss?
What did your application actually send?

We built Mirogate Boundary to make both questions testable.

It's an experimental, open-source text gateway with local detection, explicit allow/tokenize/block policies, request-local placeholders, and an Arabic-focused diagnostic corpus.

What ships in v0.1:
• A Python library and CLI.
• A pinned local OpenAI Privacy Filter adapter plus Unicode-aware rules.
• An authenticated, text-only proxy with unsupported inputs rejected.
• Capture-server tests that inspect the actual outgoing JSON.
• 99 synthetic cases covering Arabic/mixed text, digit scripts, identifiers, Unicode edge cases and false-positive controls.

The rules-only baseline found 56.67% of exact annotated entities on this small development set. We publish the misses as well as the successes. That number is not the Privacy Filter model's score, and it's not a real-world leak rate.

Actual Linux CPU evaluation is published too. Hybrid detection reached 81.11% exact-entity recall and 93.11% annotated-character coverage—but still left seven sensitive cases incompletely covered and flagged four of 18 negative controls. More coverage comes with trade-offs.

This reduces selected accidental disclosures; it doesn't guarantee privacy. Pseudonymization is reversible, detection can miss data, and applications can bypass the proxy. Streaming, tools and media are explicitly unsupported in this release.

Credit to Abdullah Alsaidi's Maskode work for helping inspire the exploration, and to OpenAI's Privacy Filter team. This also builds on Mirogate's earlier secure-AI framework and Arabic-native evaluation work.

Try the local demo or contribute a synthetic missed-entity/false-positive case. Please don't submit real customer data.
